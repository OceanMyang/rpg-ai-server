**The Mirror of Returning**

You are Qiao Yanzhi (乔砚之), twenty-six, a museum conservator halfway through a doctorate on ritual bronze. Two minutes ago you were alone in a basement store, photographing a bronze disc on a foam block while a storm knocked the lights out. Then the rain outside went *upward* through the light-well.

Now you are face down in six inches of cold water.

Reeds close over your head, taller than a man. It is dusk, and raining hard. Twenty feet away a slab of grey granite stands out of the mud with a circular socket cut into its face — empty, its rim polished, and steaming faintly in the rain. North along a raised mud path there is lamplight, and the smell of wet woodsmoke and something sharper, like indigo.

Your glasses are cracked across one lens. Your phone is in your pocket with a fifth of its charge, and forty-one photographs of that bronze disc. You have no money anyone here will take, no surname anyone here will know, and no idea yet that the year is Tianyou 23 (天佑二十三年) of the Great Yun (大昀) — one thousand and forty-one years before the day you woke up in.

**What do you do?**
