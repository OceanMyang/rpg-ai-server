# Mere Shrine

- **Type:** Location (shrine of [[The Lady of the Mere]])
- **Status:** Still tended, but the bell-house stands empty; [[Brother Caddock]] has vowed to topple the Bellstone
- **Location:** On the lakeshore, 10 minutes' walk north of [[Dunmere]]
- **Keepers:** [[Sister Maren]] (priestess) and [[Wren]] (acolyte)

## Description
A small round shrine of grey fieldstone with a turf roof, open on the lake side. Inside: a stone basin that should hold lake water (dry), a carved wooden figure of a woman crowned with water-lilies, and offerings — dried flowers, clay fish, little wooden boats, and lately, desperate notes pinned under pebbles ("Please. My children are thirsty.").

Beside it:
- **The bell-house** — a small timber belfry where the Rain Bell hangs between Midsummers. Empty. Its door has no lock.
- **The Bellstone** — a tall standing stone at the old waterline with an iron bracket, where the bell is hung and rung on Midsummer's Eve. The water has retreated forty yards from it across cracked mud. Ringing the bell here is the only way to renew the pact.
- **Maren's cottage** — a two-room stone cottage behind the shrine, where Maren and Wren live.

## Clues (for investigation)
- The bell-house rope was **cut clean with a knife** — not frayed, not snapped. Someone with a knife, or someone staging a theft.
- No cart or hoof marks on the path that night (Maren told Corwin so). The bell weighs about 30 pounds: someone carried it.
- In the cottage: the **shrine ledgers** — records of every Bell Night for three centuries (the oldest volumes were carried out of drowned Wendle with the bell). In the latest volume, **three pages are torn out**: the entries for 1097, 1047, and 997 — the fiftieth years. (Wits roll to notice the stubs.)
- Maren keeps the torn pages in a tin box in the chest at the foot of her bed.
- **The hearthstone** in the cottage sits slightly uneven. Beneath it, wrapped in an old wool habit, is [[The Rain Bell]]. (Found by searching the hearth specifically, or on a Wits roll while searching the cottage.)

## History
- Built in Dunmere's founding year to replace the drowned chapel of [[Old Wendle]].
- The night before Midsummer's Eve, 1147 — the Rain Bell disappears from the bell-house. Bell Night (Midsummer's Eve) cannot be held.
