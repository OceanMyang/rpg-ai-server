# Merrow Lake

- **Type:** Location (lake)
- **Status:** Receding — down about twelve feet, ringed by a wide band of cracked mud; the rooftops of [[Old Wendle]] are emerging in the north shallows
- **Location:** The heart of [[Merrowdale]]; [[Dunmere]] sits on its east shore
- **Dweller:** [[The Lady of the Mere]]

## Description
Normally a broad, green, lily-fringed lake two miles long. Now a shrunken pool of dark green water inside a ring of mud baked into curling plates. Dead fish, bones, and lost things lie on the flats. The water is unnaturally cold for high summer, and at night mist gathers on it even in the heat.

## Features
- **The mudflats** — hard and cracked near the old shoreline; soft, sucking mud nearer the water. Crossing quickly takes a Grace roll; going slowly on safe paths takes twice as long. [[Wren]] knows the safe paths.
- **The Bellstone** — at the old waterline by the [[Mere Shrine]].
- **The Vance Weir** — the stone dam at the south end, built by the Vance family. Its sluice is shut; the mill channel below it is dry.
- **The north shallows** — where [[Old Wendle]] stands half out of the water.

## Rules of the Lake (GM)
- The Lady knows whatever happens on her water and her shore.
- Anyone who wades or swims in the lake **at night** may be drawn under (Might roll to break free, −1). A drowning-charm from [[Granny Nettle]] protects the wearer for one night.
- On the night of the full moon (Day 3), [[Tam Ashby]]'s singing carries across the water, and he can be spoken to at the water's edge.
- Speaking respectfully to the water at the Bellstone may draw an answer — ripples, sudden cold, a voice. Roll `oracle unlikely` by day, `oracle even` at night. Insults or threats make the water churn and turn icy.

## History
- ~160 years ago — the Vance Weir raises the lake and drowns Old Wendle.
- Since Midsummer's Eve, 1147 — the lake has shrunk steadily without rain.
