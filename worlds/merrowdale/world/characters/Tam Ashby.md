# Tam Ashby

- **Type:** Character (human man — changed; looks 19, truly 69)
- **Status:** Alive, after a fashion — the Voice of [[The Lady of the Mere]], beneath [[Merrow Lake]] for fifty years
- **Location:** Under the lake. He can appear at the water's edge at night, and in the drowned chapel of [[Old Wendle]].
- **Role:** The Lady's Voice: she speaks to mortals through him

## Description
A young man with pale, water-smooth skin, hair that drifts as if underwater even in the air, and eyes green as lake-weed. His voice is beautiful and carries across water. Wet footprints follow him and fade. He wears the rotting Midsummer finery of fifty years ago.

## Persona
- Dreamy, gentle, melancholy, a little strange — time has blurred for him; he calls the fifty years "a long evening".
- Sometimes speaks as himself ("I"), sometimes for the Lady ("We"). When she speaks through him his eyes go wholly green, and he cannot lie.
- Starved for news of the living. Asks about the village, and about Hester: "Is Hester still angry with me?"

## Knowledge
- The whole truth of the pact (see [[The Lady of the Mere]]).
- When a new Voice goes down, he will be released. He doesn't know what that will do to him.
- He sings to [[Wren]] in her dreams because the Lady bids him call the next Voice. He hates it.

## Secrets (GM only)
- If released, he walks out of the lake at dawn and ages his true 69 years over a single day — then lives out whatever years are left to him as an old man.
- He wants to be free, but not at the price of a child.
- He still loves Hester ([[Granny Nettle]]). He begged the Lady to spare her spring in Thornwood.

## Relationships
- [[The Lady of the Mere]] — mistress, companion, jailer.
- [[Granny Nettle]] — Hester, his sweetheart.
- [[Wren]] — the child he's made to call.

## History
- 1097, Midsummer's Eve — told the truth by old Mother Orla, he rang the bell in place of the young acolyte and walked into the lake of his own free will.
- About 30 days before Day 1 — begins singing to Wren in her dreams.
