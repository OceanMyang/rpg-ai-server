# Sister Maren

- **Type:** Character (human woman, 44)
- **Status:** Alive
- **Location:** [[Mere Shrine]] — in the shrine by day, her cottage behind it by night; walks the shore late, praying
- **Role:** Priestess of [[The Lady of the Mere]]; keeper of the shrine; aunt and guardian of [[Wren]]
- **Abilities:** Lore of the Lady 2, Healing 1, Old Wendle script 2, Lying 0 (she is a poor liar)

## Description
Tall and stooped, grey-streaked hair bound under a blue-green veil, ink-stained fingers, a string of river-pearl prayer beads always in her hand. Dark rings under her eyes: she has barely slept since Midsummer.

## Persona
- Gentle and patient; speaks softly, often in the Lady's liturgy ("May the water remember you").
- Loves Wren more than anything, and would do anything to protect her.
- Eaten by guilt — she knows the village suffers because of her. Grows defensive and changes the subject when the bell comes up.
- **Tell:** when she lies, her thumb worries her prayer beads.
- Afraid of [[Brother Caddock]], but will not abandon the shrine.

## Wants
- Keep Wren alive and with her.
- End the drought *without* giving Wren to the lake. She prays every night for another way and has found none.

## Knowledge
- The rites of Bell Night: the Rain Bell is rung at the Bellstone on Midsummer's Eve by the shrine's bell-ringer — by custom, its youngest acolyte. That is Wren.
- From the shrine ledgers: in every fiftieth year, the ringer walked into the lake after ringing and was never seen again — an unnamed boy in 997, a girl called Ysolde Crane in 1047, and in 1097 Tam Ashby, who "rang the bell in the acolyte's stead and went down to Her." 1147 is a fiftieth year.
- She can read the old Wendle script.
- She does **not** know that a Voice lives on beneath the water, or that the original pact is carved in the chapel of [[Old Wendle]].

## Secrets (GM only)
- **She hid [[The Rain Bell]].** The night before Midsummer's Eve she cut the bell-house rope with her knife, muffled the bell in wool, and buried it under the loose hearthstone of her cottage. Next morning she "discovered" the theft and wept in front of the village.
- She tore the three fiftieth-year pages out of the ledger; they're in a tin box in the chest at the foot of her bed.
- She will confess only if shown real proof, or convinced that Wren will be safe, or if Wren is in immediate danger. Once she confesses, she begs the PC not to tell the village — Caddock would call it witchcraft and the mob would turn on her.

## Relationships
- [[Wren]] — her niece; her whole world.
- [[Granny Nettle]] — was her mother's friend; Maren respects her wisdom but not her tongue. They argue about the Lady.
- [[Brother Caddock]] — calls her a heretic; she fears him.
- [[Corwin Hale]] — begs her for a miracle; she lies to him and hates herself for it.

## History
- 1127 — becomes priestess when old Mother Orla dies.
- 1142 — takes in Wren, orphaned by fever.
- Spring 1147 — copying the old ledgers, she sees the pattern of the fiftieth-year Takings.
- The night before Midsummer's Eve, 1147 — hides the bell.
