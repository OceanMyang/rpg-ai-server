# Old Wendle

- **Type:** Location (drowned village)
- **Status:** Half-submerged and emerging as the lake recedes. The chapel door is still underwater until Day 3 (see Clocks in `game/state.md`).
- **Location:** The north shallows of [[Merrow Lake]], an hour's walk across the mudflats from [[Dunmere]]

## Description
The broken walls and caved-in roofs of a village drowned 160 years ago, draped in black weed and freshwater mussels, standing in knee- to waist-deep water and stinking mud. Fish flop in the pools inside ruined houses. At the center, the chapel's slate roof and square tower lean above the water, a rusted weathervane shaped like a fish on top.

## The Chapel
- **The door:** heavy oak, iron-bound, locked. The rusted key [[Wren]] found in the mud fits it. Otherwise force it (Might −1) or climb in through a hole in the roof (Grace −1; on a failure, a fall into cold, deep water inside).
- **Inside:** dark, waist-deep, bitterly cold water; pews rotted to stumps; the smell of centuries of silt.
- **The Pact Stone:** the altar, carved with the original terms of the pact in the old Wendle script, under a crust of silt. Reading it takes a Wits roll, or a rubbing or careful copy brought to [[Sister Maren]] or [[Granny Nettle]], who can both read the old script. It says:
  > *Come to my shore and speak to me, and I will answer with rain.*
  > *Ring my bell at midsummer, that I may know you remember.*
- The Lady's presence is strong here. [[Tam Ashby]] may appear in the chapel at any hour if called by name, or at night unbidden.

## Dangers
- Rotten beams and sudden deep holes (Grace rolls when moving fast or in the dark).
- **A drowned thing** (rating 2): the corpse of a Wendle villager, animated by the lake's cold. It ignores anyone the Lady favors but attacks those who desecrate the chapel or come at night with fire and iron.

## History
- ~400 years ago — the pact is carved on the altar stone by Wendle's founders.
- ~160 years ago — drowned when the Vance Weir raised the lake. The Rain Bell was carried out; the altar stone was too heavy and was left behind.
