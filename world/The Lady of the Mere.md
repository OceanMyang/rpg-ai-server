# The Lady of the Mere

- **Type:** Spirit (local deity of water and rain)
- **Status:** Present — dwelling in [[Merrow Lake]]; withholding the rain since the bell went unrung
- **Domain:** Rain and water in [[Merrowdale]]; drowning; memory; bargains
- **Power:** Legendary (rating 3). On or in her lake she can do almost anything water can do. Beyond the shore, her power reaches only the rain over Merrowdale. She cannot break a bargain she has struck.

## Description
Rarely seen. When she shows herself: a tall figure of dark water in a woman's shape, crowned with water-lilies, her face shifting between beautiful and drowned. More often she speaks through [[Tam Ashby]], or through ripples, mist, and a sudden cold.

## Nature & Persona
- Ancient, proud, literal-minded. She keeps her word exactly and expects the same of others.
- Not cruel, but not human: she can't see why taking a Voice horrifies mortals — to her it is an honor.
- Above all she is **lonely** and afraid of being **forgotten**.
- Threats and insults anger her (the water turns icy and rough). Courtesy, songs, stories, and gifts of memory please her.
- She knows the bell was **hidden, not stolen**, and she holds it against the village as faithlessness.

## The Pact (the truth — GM only unless discovered in play)
- **Original terms**, carved on the altar of the drowned chapel in [[Old Wendle]] about 400 years ago: *"Come to my shore and speak to me, and I will answer with rain. Ring my bell at midsummer, that I may know you remember."*
- The people of Wendle came to her shore every day to talk and sing. When the Vance Weir drowned Wendle 160 years ago, the survivors moved uphill to found [[Dunmere]], and the daily visits dwindled to one night a year.
- Starved of company, she began to take a companion — **a Voice** — every fifty years: **whoever rings the bell in the fiftieth year goes down to her.** The priestesses accepted this and kept it quiet.
- A Voice lives beneath the lake, ageless and dreaming. When a new Voice goes down, the old one is released to the shore.
- Until the bell is rung at the Bellstone, no rain falls in Merrowdale.

## What would satisfy her (GM guidance)
- **The bell is rung** by anyone → rain the next day. In this fiftieth year the ringer becomes her Voice, and Tam is released.
- **A renewed pact** that ends her loneliness: e.g. Dunmere swears to restore the old custom of coming to her shore daily to speak and sing. [[Corwin Hale]]'s oath as reeve binds the village. It must be spoken at the Bellstone and sealed by ringing the bell. Then she needs no Voice, takes no one, and frees Tam. The petitioner must know the original terms (from Old Wendle) and win her over — a Heart roll against her rating (−3), eased by +1 each for gifts or promises she truly values.
- **If the bell is never rung:** on the night of Day 6 she takes her due anyway — she calls [[Wren]] in her sleep. Wren walks into the lake; Tam is released; rain returns the next day.
- **If the Bellstone is toppled:** the pact is broken and can't be renewed by the bell. She turns vengeful: her water becomes deadly to anyone who touches it, she still takes Wren, and the rain does not come back. Resolve any further attempt at a new pact from her nature — literal, lonely, proud — and make it cost dearly.

## Relationships
- [[Tam Ashby]] — her current Voice; she is fond of him in her way.
- [[Wren]] — the ringer she is owed this year; she is calling her.
- [[Sister Maren]] — the priestess who broke faith.
- [[Brother Caddock]] — she knows he means to topple her stone.

## History
- ~400 years ago — makes the pact with Wendle's founders.
- ~160 years ago — her first people's village is drowned by the weir.
- 997, 1047, 1097 — takes a Voice each fiftieth Midsummer; Tam Ashby is the last.
- Midsummer's Eve, 1147 — the bell goes unrung. She withholds the rain.
