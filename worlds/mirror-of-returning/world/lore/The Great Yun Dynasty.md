# The Great Yun Dynasty (大昀)

- **Type:** Lore — the age the player character has fallen into
- **Now:** the twenty-third year of the Tianyou era (天佑二十三年), ninth month

An empire of rice, rivers and rain, ruled from **Yandu (堰都)**, eleven hundred miles north-east of here and about as relevant to [[Luyang Town]] as the moon.

## What the Empire is
- Prefectures (郡) under a magistrate, counties under a clerk, villages under whoever owns the water. [[Cangping Prefecture]] is a poor one: indigo, coarse porcelain, reed paper, and a river that floods.
- Taxes are grain and cloth, collected in the eighth month, and the ninth month is when people find out what they have left.
- The Emperor's writ is real in the cities and a rumour in the reeds.

## What the Empire is not
It is not in charge of the sects. The compact, four hundred years old and unwritten, runs both ways: **sects do not sit in the magistrate's chair, and the magistrate does not ask what happens on the mountain.** A Golden Core elder who kills a hundred villagers has committed no crime the Empire will prosecute; another sect might, if it wants the mountain.

This is the single most important thing for a modern person to understand, and it will take them a while: **there is no one to call.** No police, no court that outranks a cultivator, no appeal. Justice here is somebody stronger deciding to care.

## Living in it
- Everyone works. A dye-yard widow, a reed-cutter, a kiln-digger — dawn to dusk, and a bad season means selling a child's labour to the next county.
- Writing is for clerks, monks and cultivators. Roughly one adult in fifteen reads.
- Medicine is decoctions, needles, prayer, and a barber with a hot iron. A modern person who boils water, washes hands, splints a break and does not bleed a fever patient will save lives and be called a sorcerer for it.
- Strangers are asked three things: your surname, your county, and who will vouch for you. The player character has no good answer to any of them, and that — not monsters — is what nearly kills them in the first week.

## Calendar
Twelve months, the year beginning in early spring. Days are dawn (卯), morning, noon (午), afternoon, dusk (酉), night, and the watches of the night are called by drum. The ninth month is late autumn: cold rain, then frost.
